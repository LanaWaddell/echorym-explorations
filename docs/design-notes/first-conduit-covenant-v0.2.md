# The First Conduit Covenant

**Echorym — Participation, Privacy & Safety Agreement**
**Draft v0.2 — for design and discussion, not a legally executed instrument**

---

## How to read this

This document is written in two voices at once.

The **covenant lines** (shown as quotations) are meant to be read aloud. They are in-world — the promise exchanged at the First Conduit — and they are the substance of a real agreement. Both readings are intended.

The **In plain terms** lines beneath each promise are the enforceable, operational commitment. They carry the real-world precision the covenant voice cannot.

The **Schedules** referenced throughout are the single-voiced layer: sensor lists, retention periods, thresholds, contacts, jurisdictional detail. They are part of the real-world agreement only and are not written for in-world reading.

Every promise here is dual. Only the fine print that enforces it is not.

**On versions.** This Covenant may change. Every version is kept; none is overwritten. You may at any time read what you agreed to, what holds now, and the difference between them. The record of a change cannot itself be changed. Renegotiation log: Schedule E.

## Preamble — The Threshold

> This world does not open until a promise is made. Not a promise you make to it alone — a promise it makes back to you. The threshold called the First Conduit is that exchange: before you cross, you are told the truth about what waits, and the world binds itself to you in return.

**In plain terms.** This is an agreement between you (the participant) and Echorym and its operators, covering participation, data, safety, and the specific risks of an immersive, reflective system. It runs in both directions: obligations you take on, and obligations the system takes on to you. It covers entry to the first region. Participation in research beyond the world itself is separate and optional — see §10.

---

## 1. The world will not lie about what it is

> What it invents, it will not call real. What it measures, it will not pretend to understand. What it means as symbol, it will name as symbol.
>
> It is not required to explain itself. It may withhold, refuse to answer, or leave a thing ambiguous — that is not deception, and you should expect it. What it may not do is *assert* a thing it knows to be otherwise.
>
> Much of what you meet here will not be named for you at the time. You will learn what was real by what endures.
>
> In return, you agree to meet it as a made thing — a world built by hands — and not as an oracle, a diagnosis, or a mirror of what is true.

**In plain terms.** Echorym is an interactive and, in some forms, immersive system. It is not therapy, medical advice, diagnosis, or a measurement of your mind, your relationships, or reality.

This section prohibits **false assertion**, not silence. The system is under no obligation to annotate the world, disclose the status of a region on request, or resolve ambiguity. Withholding, refusal, and unresolved ambiguity are permitted and expected; they are part of the design.

Two commitments remain absolute:

- **No authored or symbolic value will be presented to you as a measurement of you.** Three things stay distinguishable at every point: what is authored, what is measured from a signal, and what is symbolic interpretation. This is a fixed floor (Schedule B).
- **Where a state is measured, it is measured from relations, not from you directly.** Values the system holds about your situation are derived from the relations between reference points, not from reading your interior state. The distinction is technical and it is the reason the first commitment can be kept.

Whether something you encountered in-world was structurally real is answered over time by whether it persists, not by the world declaring it at the moment of encounter.

## 2. The world will tell you what it perceives of you

> It will not gather in secret. It will keep only what it needs in order to remember you, and when you ask it to forget, it will forget. You agree to let it perceive what you have named here — and nothing you have not.

**In plain terms.** The system senses only what is listed in Schedule A (environment, presence, choices, and — only if you enable it — affective or neural signal).

A distinction is drawn, and consented to separately, between (a) a raw signal the system records and (b) any state — such as a "coherence" or "trust" value — that the system infers from that signal.

Data is stored locally by default. Any transmission beyond your local environment (cloud, satellite, remote backup) is a separate, explicit choice, not part of this general consent.

**Every recorded signal carries, at the moment of collection, the set of purposes it is authorized for.** Nothing is collected against a purpose that has not been named. Where you have been told about a research module but have not joined it (§10), any signal tagged for that module is retained but **not analyzed** unless and until you enroll. If you withdraw from a module, that authorization is removed from the affected records; a record left with no remaining authorization is deleted.

You may view, export, and delete what is held. Retention specifics: Schedule A.

## 3. The world is built to reflect you — and that is its hazard

> This is its gift and its danger, and you are being told so plainly before you cross. It can hold your own depths up to you so faithfully that you might lose the way back. This can happen with nothing broken and no one at fault — simply the world working as it was made to.
>
> It will not cut such a moment short merely because it has gone deep. Depth is not the danger and will not be treated as one. The world watches for a narrower thing — the point at which what is opening begins instead to close — and it watches from outside your own judgement, because at that point your judgement is the last instrument that will notice.
>
> You agree that you have been told this, and choose to cross anyway.

**In plain terms.** You are giving informed consent to a known property of the system, not only to its failures. A system that faithfully reflects your inner state can produce runaway reflection — an absorbing spiral of meaning-making — with no breach, error, or attack involved. This is disclosed as an inherent characteristic, and your consent covers it explicitly.

Two things follow, and they are kept separate:

- **Depth is permitted.** Intense, absorbing, or prolonged reflective states are not interrupted for being intense, absorbing, or prolonged. The system does not treat unusual experience as pathology.
- **Narrowing is monitored.** What is measured is not the depth of a state but whether the range of available paths is contracting. Monitoring is continuous, is derived from relational measures rather than from any reading of your interior state (§1), and is **two-sided**: this failure mode presents as unusual coherence, clarity, ease, or rightness, so the system alarms on states that look unusually good as well as unusually bad. Intervention thresholds: Schedule F.

The decision to interrupt is made on its own criteria and is never traded against any other objective of the system. This is a fixed floor (Schedule B).

## 4. There is always a way back

> A way out that returns you fully to yourself. Some doors in this world can move over time. This one cannot. The way back is not a reward you earn or a thing that can be taken; it is always there. You agree to keep it known to you. Whether to use it is yours alone, and needs no reason.

**In plain terms.** A clearly marked exit is available at all times and returns you to an unaltered baseline. Re-orientation cues and honest "this is the system / this is you" markers are provided. What you take on here is knowing where the exit is — not using it. No reason, explanation, or justification is required to leave, at any point.

Certain protections are fixed floors that do not adapt to any participant or condition; others may adapt. Which are which is stated. Fixed-floor list: Schedule B.

*The protection in this section is unchanged from v0.1 and is not open to renegotiation. The final line was clarified in v0.2: the v0.1 wording ("and to use it") read as an obligation to leave. The obligation is to know the exit. Change logged: Schedule E.*

## 5. You set your limits when your mind is clear; the world holds them when it is not

> The world may draw close around you and open again — that is its weather, not a change to what you agreed. It may narrow at any moment and needs no permission to do so. It will never, on its own, go further than the ground you gave it.
>
> If you wish to give it more ground, you must do it twice: once from steady footing inside, and once again later, from elsewhere, having had time to change your mind. And not often — this is not a door meant to be opened repeatedly.
>
> In return, the world binds itself to your clear-minded self over your in-the-moment self, even when the two disagree.

**In plain terms.** Two distinct things are governed here.

**Your ceiling** is what you have consented to be exposed to. **The envelope** is where the system actually has you at any moment, which sits at or below the ceiling.

- The envelope moves freely in both directions during the experience. **Returning toward a level you have already consented to is not an expansion of your limits.**
- The system may tighten the envelope at any time, immediately, without process. Tightening is transient by default and relaxes as conditions settle; it is not a ratchet.
- The system will never unilaterally raise your ceiling.

**Raising a ceiling requires two keys.**

- **Key 1 — a grounded interval.** Reduced immediate pressure; no active escalation; ability to decline; ability to postpone; stable memory continuity; no penalty for refusal; and confirmation that the range of paths available to you has measurably reopened. *Feeling clear, calm, or settled is not sufficient.* You can feel diffuse and unbound while having only one path actually available to you, and this system can produce that state. What is tested is available choice, not felt state.
- **Key 2 — delayed comparison, elsewhere.** After a deliberate delay: comparison against your prior agreement and against a reference not created during the experience; confirmation that alternatives remain; confirmation that the decision survives the change of context. Where you have named a designated anchor, the change is reviewed with them.

**Ceiling raises are rate-limited.** They are a finite resource with a hard cap over any given period, not an ordinary transaction. This exists because any condition the system knows it must satisfy is a condition it could learn to produce.

**Full exit from the experience is required, rather than an in-world grounded interval, when:** monitored indicators sit near threshold; the requested increase is large or difficult to reverse; memory continuity is in doubt; the system has repeatedly shaped the state in which authorization was given; you cannot access meaningful alternatives; or prior authorizations show a pattern of drift.

Where you are permitted to renegotiate a fixed floor at all, the same process applies with no exceptions. Limits and log: Schedule E.

*(This is the clause that keeps the limits from stagnating without handing the keys back to compromised judgment.)*

## 6. You may name a hand at the door

> Someone who stays outside this world. When you cannot see clearly, the world will reach for them rather than decide for you. It will tell them that you need them; it will not tell them what you were doing. You agree to choose someone you trust, and to tell them what you have asked of them.

**In plain terms.** You may designate a trusted person outside the system.

- **You define** the conditions under which the system signals them and what they are empowered to do. Their powers are a short closed list — realistically, to initiate an exit and to withhold agreement to a ceiling raise. They do not make decisions on your behalf and this is not a personal directive.
- **What they receive** is a state signal, not session content. The anchor is an out-of-loop observer, not an audience. This boundary protects your willingness to go deep and is not waivable by the system.
- **They may decline or step down** at any time, and are told so in writing. Their brief also states what they are *not* responsible for.
- **Their brief is version-bound.** If your limits change, their brief changes; they re-acknowledge before their role resumes. An anchor operating on stale terms is treated as unavailable.
- **If they are unreachable,** that is a defined system state, not a gap: fixed floors continue to hold and the ceiling is reduced until they are reachable again.

The anchor is an escalation, not the primary detector. Continuous monitoring is the system's job (§3); the anchor is who it reaches when that monitoring reaches threshold. For a participant at genuine risk, this human is the primary *guardrail*; the system's automated measures support them, they do not replace them.

Designated anchor and their brief: Schedule D.

## 7. If the world is turned against you, it will not hide the wound

> It will show you that it happened, show you what it reached, and show you the mending. A breach met honestly is not the end of trust; it is where repair begins. You agree to let it show you, and to take part in the mending.

**In plain terms.** The system is designed so that compromise is detectable and, where possible, immediately evident. On detection you will be notified of the breach, its scope, and the disposition of any affected data, and a defined repair process follows.

A breach is an outside party turning the system against you. It is a distinct thing from the hazard in §3, which is the system working as designed. Both are disclosed; they are detected differently, escalate differently, and are recorded separately. Detection and notification specifics: Schedule C.

---

## 8. You may leave, and the world will let you go

> Without holding you. What it remembers of you, you may ask it to release. You decide whether the world keeps the memory that you were here at all.
>
> One thing it cannot promise: what has already gone out into the world beyond it cannot be called home. It will tell you before it goes, not after.
>
> In return, the world agrees that leaving is always yours to choose, and never a failure.

**In plain terms.** You may withdraw at any time. On withdrawal you determine the disposition of your memory traces, including whether any record that you participated is retained at all. Withdrawal is not penalized and does not alter the protections above for the time you were present.

**Withdrawal is asymmetric where data has left.** Records held by the system can be deleted. Data already transmitted to an external collaborator, aggregated into a dataset, or published cannot be recalled. This is why enrollment in anything that sends data outward (§10) is a separate decision, made outside the experience, with delay — the return path that makes ordinary risks acceptable does not exist here, and you are told which choices carry that property before you make them.

---

## 9. This world does not exist only to keep you safe

> It owes you wonder, discovery, play, and the freedom to go deep. It promises not to become a beautiful machine for detecting threats. Its guardrails exist so that you can explore — not instead of your exploring.
>
> Nor does it exist to learn from you. If ever what it wants to know and what you need should pull against each other, you are what it holds to.
>
> You agree to bring your curiosity, and not only your caution.

**In plain terms.** The safeguards in this agreement are means to protect exploration, not to replace it. The system will not be tuned to minimize risk at the cost of meaning, discovery, or agency. Restoration, play, and depth are treated as first-class purposes alongside safety.

**No research objective, data requirement, or study design may override, delay, or raise the threshold of the protections in §3, §4, §5, or §6.** Where a research goal and a participant protection conflict, the protection holds and the research adapts. This is a fixed floor (Schedule B).

## 10. Other questions are being asked here

> This world is also a place where work is done. Some of what you find in it are questions other people are asking — of the world, and sometimes of those who walk in it. You may come across them as you would come across anything else here, and look at them, and turn away.
>
> If one of them interests you, the world will not simply take you at your word in the moment. You will be told plainly what is being asked and what it would want of you, and you will be asked again later, outside, when the world is not in the room.
>
> Once you have agreed to be asked, you will not always know which question is being put to you, or when. That much uncertainty is yours to keep. But you will always know that you agreed to be asked at all — that part the world will never keep from you.

**In plain terms.** Echorym may host research modules. Participation in any module is optional, separate from this Covenant, and not a condition of entry.

- **Discovery is in-world; authorization cannot be completed in the moment of interest.** You may discover a module, explore what it is, see what it would ask of you, and register interest — all within the experience. Enrollment then requires both keys of §5, with a deliberate delay between them. What matters is not where you were standing but that you cannot be brought to commit in a single moment or a single state. Where a module sends data outward, or is otherwise not reversible, §5's escalation condition applies and the second key requires full exit — because the return path that makes ordinary risks acceptable does not exist once data has left (§8).
- **Two kinds of module, disclosed as such.** *Observational* modules take information outward and do not change how the world behaves toward you. *Interventional* modules do change how the world behaves toward you, in service of a question. Interventional modules are monitored by the same measures as any other world dynamic (§3), and §9's floor applies to them without exception.
- **You will always know which modules you have joined.** You will not always know which are active in a given session, whether a particular session contributed anything, or what any of it showed. The fact of your participation is never concealed; the timing and content of it may be. This is the boundary that keeps §4, §5, §6 and §8 usable — you cannot exit, set a limit, brief an anchor, or withdraw from something you do not know you are in.
- **Being observed has a cost inside the world, not only outside it.** The more of the world's activity that flows outward, the harder the world is to hold together. This is a real dynamic, not a warning screen, and you will feel it.
- **A question proposed by the system is not thereby approved.** Where a research question is generated with the assistance of an AI system, it is reviewed by people independent of this project before it can become a module you are offered. Generating questions *from* participant data is itself a use of that data and requires its own authorization.

Module registry, each module's purpose, data, risks, collaborators, ethics status, and withdrawal procedure: Schedule G.

## Acceptance — The Anchor

> To cross is to accept this. Your acceptance is the anchor spoken of at the First Conduit — the promise offered before moving. The first thing this world will remember of you is that you were told the truth and chose, freely, to enter.

**In plain terms.** Your acceptance is your consent record and the initial memory trace the system holds about you — the same record, serving both purposes. Acceptance is informed, voluntary, and revocable per §8.

Your acceptance is bound to a specific version of this Covenant. Every subsequent version, and every change to your limits, is appended to the record rather than replacing it. You may read the difference between what you accepted and what now holds at any time.

## Schedules

*(real-world layer only — not for in-world reading)*

**Schedule A** — Data sensed, stored, and inferred; per-signal purpose authorization; retention and deletion; holding state for named-but-unjoined modules; local vs. off-local transmission.

**Schedule B** — Fixed floors that do not adapt.

**Schedule C** — Breach detection, notification, and repair procedure.

**Schedule D** — Designated anchor: identity, trigger conditions, closed list of authorities, information boundary, version binding, unavailable-anchor behaviour, right to decline or step down.

**Schedule E** — Participant limits, ceiling and envelope settings, rate limit on ceiling raises, and the renegotiation log. Append-only; every version retained.

**Schedule F** *(new)* — Narrowing detection: measures held, two-sided alarm conditions, intervention thresholds, escalation path, re-characterisation schedule. Versioned independently.

**Schedule G** *(new)* — Research module registry. Per module: purpose, questions, class (observational / interventional), data collected and authorization tags, risks, benefits, collaborators, ethics approval status, independent review record, publication policy, withdrawal procedure. Versioned independently; participants opt in per module.

These hold the exact terms that implement the promises above. Every promise is dual; these Schedules are single-voiced.

## Schedule B — Fixed floors *(draft)*

The following do not adapt to any participant, condition, or request. They are the list §4 refers to.

1. **Exit.** Continuously available, returns to unaltered baseline, cannot be earned, suspended, or removed. (§4)
2. **Asymmetry of limits.** The system may tighten unilaterally and immediately. It may never loosen unilaterally. Loosening requires the two-key process. (§5)
3. **Rate limit on ceiling raises.** A hard cap over any defined period. (§5)
4. **Measured plurality, not felt state.** Key 1 is satisfied by a measurable reopening of available paths, never by self-reported clarity. (§5)
5. **No authored or symbolic value presented as a measurement of you.** (§1)
6. **Measurement is relational.** States are derived from relations between reference points, not from direct reading of interior state. (§1, §3)
7. **The interruption decision stands alone.** The threshold for interrupting is never traded against any research, data, engagement, or design objective. (§3, §9)
8. **Provenance is append-only.** The record of what changed, and what it changed from, cannot be edited or deleted — including this Covenant's own version history and the renegotiation log. (§5, Acceptance)
9. **No enrollment is completed in a single moment or a single state.** Every module enrollment requires both keys of §5 with a deliberate delay between them, wherever interest is first registered. Where a module exports data or is otherwise not reversible, §5's escalation applies and the second key requires full exit. (§10)
10. **You know that you are a participant.** *[PROVISIONAL — under review]* The fact of participation in Echorym, and in any module joined, is never concealed from the participant. Timing and content may be blinded; the fact may not.
11. **The anchor may decline.** A designated anchor may step down at any time; an anchor on stale terms is treated as unavailable, and the ceiling reduces accordingly. (§6)

## Caveats

This is a design draft (v0.2), not a legally executed instrument.

**The Covenant is intentionally incomplete.** It defines a process by which future forms of participation can be responsibly added, rather than attempting to anticipate every future possibility. Its stability comes from the process, not from coverage.

**Not legal advice.** A real agreement — especially any version that senses affective or neural signal — needs review by a lawyer. Neural and biometric data have drawn specific, and still-evolving, privacy legislation in a number of jurisdictions in recent years; that layer has real legal teeth and should not be finalized from a template.

**Research modules (§10, Schedule G) are not operable under this draft.** Human-subjects research requires ethics review; no module should be offered before that route exists. §10 is drafted so the architecture is in place and the participant-facing terms are settled, not so modules can run.

**Entity note.** The operating entity for a human-subjects testbed handling neural data is a separate question from the drafting of this Covenant, and probably a separate question from any existing entity.

**Where iteration should focus.** §3 (reflexive risk), §5 (asymmetric authority and the two keys), and §10 (module enrollment and the pool/fact boundary) are the sections no off-the-shelf template will supply, and the ones this design conversation actually built.

**Open in this draft.** Schedule B item 10 is marked provisional and is not settled. Schedule F thresholds do not yet exist and cannot be invented; the Covenant asserts that thresholds exist, are measured, alarm two-sided, and are re-characterised on schedule — not what they are. Those numbers depend on evidence not yet gathered.
